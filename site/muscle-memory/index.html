Case study 01 · Product design + AI

Muscle Memory

A strength training app I designed, built and shipped solo with AI as my engineering partner. Then I extended it into a gym platform that turns any facility's equipment into personalized workouts.

Role
Founder, product designer, creative director
Platform
Progressive web app
Stack
React, Supabase, Netlify, Claude API
Live
mymusclememory.app

The problem

Serious lifters were tracking in notes apps and spreadsheets.

Most fitness apps chase beginners with streaks and badges. Lifters running real programs like PHAT, Upper/Lower or Push Pull Legs needed something faster: log a set in one tap, rotate programs on schedule, and never lose their history.

My approach

Design it like a product, build it like a studio of one.

I owned every layer: brand, design system, UX flows, specs and launch. I wrote detailed product specs and used Claude Code to build them, reviewing each branch on a live preview before it shipped. Design decisions came from my own training floor and beta tester feedback.

Design system

Built for a dim gym and a sweaty thumb.

A dark interface with two signal colors: ember for power days, glacier for hypertrophy days. Barlow Condensed keeps numbers big and scannable between sets.

Key features

Small interactions, solved carefully.

01

Program rotation

Rotate whole programs on a schedule. Every new cycle restarts at Day 1, so nobody loses their place.

02

Long-press supersets

Hold an exercise to link it to the next. Three gestures kept distinct: scroll, open and link, tuned for left- and right-handed lifters.

03

Exercise swap

Machine taken? Swap it mid-workout. A SWAPPED badge keeps the history honest.

04

Recall coaching notes

Inline notes read your last session and tell you what to do next: move up, hold, or back off a stalled lift.

05

Local-first with cloud backup

Workouts save on the phone and work offline. A one-time email code turns on backup and sync across devices, no password.

06

Three-door onboarding

Get a recommended program, build your own, or import one. Experience level shapes how much the app explains.

How AI is used

AI on both sides: building the product and inside the product.

Building it

Claude Code as engineering partner

I write the specs, Claude Code writes the code on separate branches, and I review every change on a live preview before it reaches production. I also run audit passes: AI reports bugs and UX pain points first, and nothing gets fixed until I've reviewed the findings.

Inside it

Photo-to-program import

Snap a photo of a whiteboard or a coach's program and Claude reads it into an editable draft. Pasted text and PDFs are parsed right on the phone. My rule: the AI parses, it never authors. The lifter stays in control of every set.

Bring a program in, review every day and exercise, then start it now or save it for later.

Extension · Gym platform

America's Gym: when 100+ machines outgrow a personal tour.

The owner of America's Gym in Wheeling, IL used to walk every new member around the floor and show them each machine. After growing past 100 pieces of equipment, that stopped being possible, and new members felt intimidated by gear they didn't know how to use.

I designed a facility layer for Muscle Memory. The gym supplies its equipment list and the app builds workouts from what's actually on the floor, with the owner's own demo videos as tutorials.

Facility-aware workouts

Equipment tutorials

Gym branding, one product

Each facility gets its look, always "powered by Muscle Memory."

The pitch prototype, in the gym's own brand. Every machine has a QR sticker that opens its tutorial. Videos are stand-ins until the owner's demos go in.

Business model

Free, on purpose.

EVERYTHING INCLUDED

Programs, rotation, coaching notes, imports, weekly reports and full history. No paywall, no subscription.

THE GROWTH PLAY

I built a paid tier on Stripe, then removed it to grow the user base first. Gyms are the path to revenue: the facility layer gives every member the app through their membership.
